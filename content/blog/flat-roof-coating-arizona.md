# Flat Roof Coating in Arizona: How the Job Should Be Done

**A flat roof coating is 80 percent preparation and 20 percent product. On an Arizona low-slope roof, the two decisions that determine whether the coating lasts eight years or two are the moisture survey before anything is applied, and whether you use silicone or acrylic where water ponds. Coating a wet substrate seals the moisture in and rots the deck underneath while the roof looks brand new from the street. This page is about the job itself.**

If you are deciding whether a coating is the right call at all, or comparing cost against replacement, start with [flat roof replacement cost in Arizona](/blog/flat-roof-replacement-cost-arizona/). For reflectivity, energy savings, rebates, and code, see [cool roof coating in Arizona](/blog/cool-roof-coating-arizona/). This one covers what a competent application actually involves.

## Why is a moisture survey non-negotiable?

Because coating traps whatever is underneath it.

A flat roof that has been leaking has saturated insulation or foam somewhere, and that water does not leave on its own. Seal a reflective membrane over it and the moisture stays against the deck permanently. The roof looks perfect and the structure below it degrades.

An infrared or capacitance moisture scan finds those wet areas before anything is applied. Wet sections get cut out and replaced; dry sections get coated. A contractor proposing a coating on a roof more than ten years old without any moisture assessment is guessing, and the guess is expensive when it is wrong.

The [NRCA's technical resources](https://www.nrca.net/roofing-guidelines/resources) treat substrate condition as the determining factor for any recover system, which is exactly what a coating is.

## What does proper preparation involve?

Four steps, and skipping any of them is why coatings fail early.

1. **Cleaning.** Power washing to remove chalk, dirt, and failed coating. Adhesion to a dirty surface is not adhesion.
2. **Repairing.** Seams, blisters, cracks, and open penetrations get addressed before coating, not covered by it.
3. **Priming.** Required on most substrates. Skipping primer is the single most common corner cut, and it shows up as peeling in year two or three.
4. **Detailing.** Penetrations, parapet transitions, scuppers, and equipment curbs get reinforced with fabric or mastic. These are where flat roofs leak, and a roller pass over them is not detailing.

On Arizona flat roofs, the detail work around evaporative coolers and HVAC curbs matters disproportionately, because that equipment is heavy, it vibrates, and it has been sitting there for years.

## Silicone or acrylic for an Arizona flat roof?

The honest answer depends on ponding, and most Arizona flat roofs pond somewhere.

**Silicone** holds up under standing water and resists ultraviolet degradation well. It is the right choice where water sits after monsoon storms, which is most low-slope Arizona roofs at some point in their life. It costs more, and it holds dirt, which reduces reflectivity over time without reducing waterproofing.

**Acrylic** costs less, stays cleaner, and reflects well. It does not tolerate standing water. On a roof with genuinely good drainage and no ponding, it is a reasonable and cheaper choice.

**Elastomeric** is a category term rather than a chemistry, and both of the above can be described that way. If a bid says only "elastomeric," ask which chemistry.

The practical rule: if water stands anywhere on your roof more than 48 hours after rain, use silicone. The reflectivity tradeoff is real but secondary, and the [EPA's cool roof guidance](https://www.epa.gov/heatislands/using-cool-roofs-reduce-heat-islands) and [Department of Energy cool roof criteria](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/purchasing-energy-efficient-cool-roof-products) cover what reflectivity buys you in a hot dry climate.

## How thick should the coating be?

Thickness is the specification, and it is where cheap bids hide.

Coating is sold and applied by mil thickness, and coverage rate determines it. A bid that names a product but not a dry mil thickness or a gallons-per-square figure is not a specification, it is a price. Two bids using the same product at different thicknesses are different jobs with different lifespans, and the thinner one will look identical on day one.

Ask for dry mil thickness in writing, and ask how many coats. Multiple thinner coats generally outperform one thick pass.

## When should an Arizona flat roof be recoated?

Inspect around year five, recoat between years seven and ten, then repeat.

That interval is shorter than manufacturer literature written for milder markets suggests, because the [NWS Phoenix office documents the sustained extreme heat and ultraviolet load](https://www.weather.gov/psr/heat) the low desert delivers. Ultraviolet exposure is what consumes a topcoat, and there is more of it here.

The failure mode to watch for is chalking: a powdery residue that comes off on your hand. That is the coating breaking down, and it is the signal to schedule a recoat while the substrate is still sound. On a foam roof, visible exposed foam means you are already late, and bare foam degrades within a season or two. See [foam roofing in Arizona](/blog/foam-roofing-arizona/).

A recoat on a maintained roof is straightforward. A recoat on a neglected one becomes a replacement.

## How do you spot a bad coating job?

Six signs, most visible within two years.

- **Peeling or delamination**, which almost always means primer was skipped or the surface was not clean.
- **Blistering**, which means moisture was trapped underneath.
- **Thin or translucent areas** where the substrate shows through, meaning coverage rate was stretched.
- **Untreated penetrations** where coating was rolled up to a vent or curb but not detailed into it.
- **Ponding in new locations**, which suggests drainage was not addressed.
- **Chalking within the first few years**, which points at product quality or thickness.

Photograph the roof after the job is finished, including the detail work at penetrations. It is the evidence you will want if a warranty conversation happens later.

## Does a coating need a permit, and what about warranty?

Recoat work that does not alter the roof system usually falls below the permit threshold, but scope decides it. Jurisdictions differ across the Valley, and [unincorporated Maricopa County](https://www.maricopa.gov/1674/Residential-Construction) rules apply outside city limits. Confirm with your contractor rather than assuming.

Coating warranties come in two parts, like every other roofing warranty: a manufacturer warranty on the product and a workmanship warranty from the applicator. Manufacturer coverage frequently requires a certified applicator and a specified thickness, which is another reason the mil spec belongs in writing. See [what a roof warranty covers in Arizona](/blog/roof-warranty-arizona-what-it-covers/).

Verify the contractor at the [Arizona Registrar of Contractors](https://roc.az.gov/before-hire) before signing. Coating is a specialty application, and the quality of the work shows up in years three through seven rather than on completion day.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does flat roof coating cost in Arizona?**
Roughly $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot depending on chemistry, thickness, and how much preparation the roof needs. Heavy prep on a neglected roof pushes it toward the top of that range.

**How long does a flat roof coating last in Arizona?**
Seven to ten years for a properly applied silicone system on a sound substrate, less for acrylic and less again where water ponds. Ultraviolet exposure here is the limiting factor.

**Can you coat over a roof that is already leaking?**
Not without addressing the leak and any trapped moisture first. Coating over saturated material seals water against the deck and causes rot that is invisible until it is structural.

**Is silicone always better than acrylic in Arizona?**
Not always, but usually. Silicone tolerates standing water and acrylic does not, and most Arizona flat roofs pond somewhere. On a roof with genuinely good drainage, acrylic costs less and stays cleaner.

**How do I know when my flat roof needs recoating?**
Chalking is the main signal, along with thinning at high-exposure areas and any visible substrate. Inspect around year five rather than waiting for a leak, because a recoat on a sound roof costs a fraction of a replacement.
