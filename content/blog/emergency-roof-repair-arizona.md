# Emergency Roof Repair in Arizona: What to Do First

**If water is actively entering your home, the order is: contain the water inside, photograph everything, get a tarp on the roof, then call for repair. Emergency tarping in the Phoenix metro typically runs $400 to $1,200 and is almost always reimbursable, because your policy requires you to prevent further damage. The repair itself can wait until daylight. The mitigation cannot, and delaying it is one of the few ways to turn a covered claim into a denied one.**

Most pages that rank for this are contractor phone numbers. This one is what to actually do in the ninety minutes before anyone arrives.

## What counts as a roofing emergency?

Four situations justify a middle-of-the-night call. Everything else waits until morning.

- **Active water entry.** Water coming through a ceiling or running down an interior wall.
- **Structural exposure.** Missing roof sections, visible decking, a hole from a tree limb or debris.
- **Sagging ceiling.** A bulging or sagging drywall ceiling holds pooled water and can collapse.
- **Electrical contact.** Water near fixtures, panels, or ceiling boxes. That is a fire and shock hazard, not a roofing problem, and it takes priority.

A stain that appeared after last week's storm is not an emergency. It is urgent, and it should be inspected within days, but paying emergency rates for it wastes money you will want for the repair.

## What do you do in the first hour?

In this order.

1. **Kill power to affected circuits** if water is anywhere near electrical.
2. **Contain the water.** Buckets, towels, and a tarp or plastic over furniture. Move what you can.
3. **Relieve a bulging ceiling.** Puncture the low point of a sagging bulge with a screwdriver and catch the water in a bucket. It feels wrong. It is correct. A controlled drain beats a ceiling collapse.
4. **Photograph and video everything**, before cleanup and with timestamps. This is your claim.
5. **Call your insurer's claim line** to open a claim number, even if you are undecided about pursuing it.
6. **Arrange tarping.**

Do not climb onto a wet Arizona tile roof in the dark. Tile breaks underfoot, and tile you break yourself is not storm damage your insurer will pay for.

## Why does tarping matter so much for your claim?

Because nearly every homeowners policy imposes a duty to mitigate. You are contractually required to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage once you know about it. Damage that accumulates after you knew and did nothing is where denials come from.

That cuts in your favor too. Reasonable mitigation costs are typically reimbursable, so the tarp is usually not money you eat. Keep the receipt and photograph the tarp in place.

Expect $400 to $1,200 for professional emergency tarping in the metro, depending on roof area, pitch, and hour. A tile roof costs more to tarp than shingle because the surface is fragile and the crew works slower.

## When is a roof emergency actually a monsoon event?

Most of the year, an Arizona roofing emergency is a plumbing problem or an aging roof finally failing. Between June 15 and September 30, it is usually wind.

The [National Weather Service defines Arizona's monsoon season as June 15 through September 30](https://www.weather.gov/fgz/monsooninfo). During it, the failure sequence is consistent: an outflow gust front lifts tile or breaks shingle seals, and the rain that follows finds the opening. The [August 9, 2026 event](https://www.weather.gov/psr/August92026) is a clean example, with an 84 mph gust measured in Buckeye and NWS documenting "roof parts blown off" across the West Valley.

If your emergency happened on a date NWS documented, note that date. It is the strongest evidence you will have that the cause was a storm rather than deterioration. Our [2026 monsoon damage guide](/blog/monsoon-roof-damage-arizona-2026/) covers documenting it.

## How do you find the leak source?

You usually cannot from inside, and that is normal.

Water travels. It enters at a flashing detail or a lifted tile, runs along the underside of the deck or down a rafter, and drops through the ceiling somewhere entirely different. The stain marks where water exited, not where it entered. Our guide to [finding a roof leak](/blog/how-to-find-a-roof-leak/) covers tracing it properly.

For the emergency itself this does not matter. Tarping covers the general area and buys time for a real diagnosis in daylight.

## How do you avoid hiring the wrong crew at 2 a.m.?

This is the genuine risk of an emergency, because urgency is exactly the condition scam operations are built around.

Verify the license even now. The [Arizona Registrar of Contractors database](https://roc.az.gov/before-hire) is public and searchable from your phone while you wait, and roofing requires a [specific license classification](https://roc.az.gov/license-classifications) that a general residential license does not cover.

Then hold three lines:

- **Authorize mitigation only.** Tarping and containment tonight. Nothing about a full replacement gets decided at 2 a.m.
- **Never sign an assignment of benefits at the door.** It hands your claim to the contractor.
- **Get the emergency charge in writing before work starts**, including whether it credits toward the eventual repair.

After a major storm, out-of-state crews arrive within days. See [storm chaser roofers in Arizona](/blog/storm-chaser-roofer-arizona/) and [Arizona roofing scams](/blog/roofing-scams-arizona/). If something goes wrong, your recourse runs through the [ROC complaint process](https://roc.az.gov/file-complaint), which requires the contractor to have been licensed at the time of the work and imposes a two year filing deadline.

## What happens after the tarp?

A tarp is a two to four week solution in Arizona, less if it takes sun and wind. Then it degrades.

Use that window for a real diagnosis and competing bids on the permanent repair. Emergency pricing should not carry into the actual work, and a contractor who tarped your roof has no automatic claim on the repair job. Permit requirements vary between incorporated cities and [unincorporated Maricopa County](https://www.maricopa.gov/1674/Residential-Construction).

Whether you need a repair or a replacement is a separate judgment, and the answer usually comes down to underlayment age rather than the visible damage. The [NRCA's technical resources](https://www.nrca.net/roofing-guidelines/resources) treat underlayment as the actual water barrier, which is why a small hole in a 20 year old roof often means more than it looks like. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does emergency roof repair cost in Arizona?**
Emergency tarping typically runs $400 to $1,200 in the Phoenix metro depending on area, pitch, and hour. The permanent repair is priced separately and should be bid competitively in daylight, not agreed to during the emergency.

**Will insurance pay for emergency tarping?**
Usually yes. Policies impose a duty to mitigate further damage, and reasonable mitigation costs are typically reimbursable. Keep receipts and photograph the tarp in place.

**Should I really puncture a sagging ceiling?**
Yes, at the low point of the bulge, with a bucket underneath. A sagging ceiling is holding water. Draining it in a controlled way is far less destructive than the collapse that follows if you wait.

**Can I tarp my own roof in Arizona?**
On a shingle roof in daylight with proper anchoring, sometimes. On tile, or at night, or in wind, no. Tile breaks underfoot, damage you cause is not covered, and falls are the actual risk. The tarp is cheaper than the emergency room.

**How long can a tarp stay on?**
Two to four weeks in Arizona conditions before UV and wind degrade it. Treat it as a countdown to the permanent repair, not a solution.
