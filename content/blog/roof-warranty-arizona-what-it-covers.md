# Roof Warranty in Arizona: What It Actually Covers

**A standard manufacturer roof warranty covers material defects and nothing else, prorated after an initial period, with labor excluded. That means a defect found in year 18 of a "lifetime" warranty may pay a few hundred dollars against a five-figure re-roof. Upgraded warranties add labor, tear-off, and disposal, but require a certified installer and registration within a set window. Your contractor's separate workmanship warranty covers installation errors, and in Arizona it is backed by a state complaint process that expires after two years.**

The word most homeowners get wrong is "lifetime." It describes the coverage period, not the payout. What determines whether a warranty is worth anything is the tier, the proration schedule, and whether labor is in scope.

## What are the two warranties, and who provides each?

Two separate documents from two separate companies, and they fail in different ways.

[CertainTeed states the distinction plainly](https://www.certainteed.com/inspiration/how-tos/my-roof-warranty-what-it-and-what-it-covers): there is "the manufacturer's (product) warranty, which covers the product against defects in the materials," and "the roofing contractor's workmanship (installation) warranty, which covers workmanship errors by the installing roofing contractor."

The manufacturer's warranty follows the product. Your contractor's warranty follows the contractor, which means it is worth exactly as much as that company's continued existence. A twenty-five year workmanship warranty from a business that closes in year four is paper.

## What does a standard manufacturer warranty actually pay?

Materials, prorated, minus labor. That is the whole answer, and it surprises people.

The mechanics work like this. Full replacement value on defective material typically applies only during an initial period after installation, often the first several years. After that, coverage declines each year toward a fraction of original material cost. By late in the term, the payout can be a small share of what the shingles cost, and shingles are usually a minority of your project total.

Labor is the omission that matters. Removing a defective roof and installing a new one is most of what you pay for. A prorated material-only settlement does not touch it.

[Owens Corning publishes its warranty tiers and what each includes](https://www.owenscorning.com/en-us/roofing/warranty), and the differences between standard and upgraded coverage are worth reading before you choose a product rather than after a failure.

## What do upgraded warranties add?

Three things, and they are the three that carry real money.

- **Labor.** Installation cost on the replacement roof.
- **Tear-off and disposal.** Removing the failed roof, which on a two-layer or tile roof is substantial.
- **A non-prorated period.** A defined window where you get full replacement value rather than a declining share.

The tradeoffs are that upgraded coverage generally requires installation by a manufacturer-certified contractor, requires registration within a window after installation, and costs more upfront.

Registration is where coverage quietly disappears. Enhanced warranties commonly require registration within 60 to 90 days of installation. Miss it and you fall back to the base limited warranty without anyone telling you. Ask your contractor for written confirmation that registration was filed, and keep it.

## What voids or excludes coverage in Arizona?

Exclusions do far more work than voiding does, and two matter especially here.

**Ventilation.** Nearly every manufacturer conditions coverage on adequate attic ventilation. In a climate where the [NWS Phoenix office documents sustained extreme heat](https://www.weather.gov/psr/heat), an underventilated attic cooks shingles from below, and premature failure gets attributed to the ventilation rather than the product. See [attic ventilation and your Arizona roof](/blog/attic-ventilation-roof-arizona/).

**Installation outside specification.** Coverage assumes the product went on as specified. [Owens Corning's Duration installation instructions](https://www.owenscorning.com/en-us/roofing/install-instructions/duration-series) prescribe fastening patterns, joint gapping, and sealing conditions. Deviations let a manufacturer route your claim back to your contractor, which is why the workmanship warranty matters so much.

Storm damage is excluded almost universally. Wind, hail, and monsoon debris are insurance events, not warranty events, and the [NRCA's technical resources](https://www.nrca.net/roofing-guidelines/resources) treat storm damage and product defect as separate categories. If you took wind this season, see [2026 monsoon roof damage](/blog/monsoon-roof-damage-arizona-2026/) rather than reaching for the warranty.

For the full picture on what actually ends coverage, see [what voids a roof warranty](/blog/what-voids-a-roof-warranty/).

## What happens to your warranty when you sell?

Most warranties allow one transfer to a subsequent owner, often within a limited window after the sale and sometimes for a fee. A second transfer usually ends coverage entirely.

The transfer has to actually be filed with the manufacturer. This is the step that gets skipped in a busy closing, and the discovery usually comes years later when someone tries to claim. If you are buying an Arizona home with a recent roof, ask for the warranty documents and written confirmation the transfer was recorded. See [does a new roof add home value in Arizona](/blog/new-roof-home-value-arizona/).

## How does Arizona back up a workmanship warranty?

With a real enforcement path that expires.

The [Arizona Registrar of Contractors accepts formal complaints](https://roc.az.gov/file-complaint) against licensed contractors. Per the ROC, for new construction complaints must be filed "within two years from the close of escrow or actual occupancy, whichever occurred first," and for remodel or repair work the two years run from the date the work was performed.

A ten year workmanship warranty does not extend that. After two years you still have a contract claim to pursue privately, but the state's leverage is gone. Both the ROC process and the Residential Contractors' Recovery Fund described in the [ROC's FAQ](https://roc.az.gov/faq) require that your contractor was licensed at the time of the work, which is the case for checking at [the ROC before you hire](https://roc.az.gov/before-hire).

## What should you do the week your roof is installed?

Five things, and they take under an hour.

1. Get both warranty documents in writing, manufacturer and workmanship.
2. Confirm in writing that the enhanced warranty was registered, if you bought one.
3. Photograph the finished roof and keep the material labels or wrappers.
4. File the contract, the final invoice, and the contractor's license number together.
5. Note the registration deadline and the ROC two-year date on a calendar.

That last item is the one nobody does, and it is the one that matters when something goes wrong in year three. Our guide to [reading a roofing warranty](/blog/how-to-read-roofing-warranty/) covers the clauses worth checking before you sign.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does a lifetime roof warranty mean I get a free new roof?**
No. Lifetime describes the coverage period, not the payout. Standard coverage is prorated material only with labor excluded, so a late-term defect claim may cover a small fraction of a replacement.

**Is an upgraded roof warranty worth the extra cost in Arizona?**
Often yes, because it adds labor, tear-off, and disposal, which are the majority of a re-roof bill. It requires a certified installer and prompt registration, so confirm both before paying for the upgrade.

**Does my roof warranty cover monsoon or hail damage?**
No. Storm damage is an insurance matter, not a warranty matter. Warranties cover manufacturing defects and, separately, installation errors.

**What happens to my warranty if my roofer goes out of business?**
The manufacturer's product warranty survives, since it comes from the manufacturer. The workmanship warranty generally does not, which is why contractor longevity is worth weighing alongside price.

**How long do I have to complain about roofing work in Arizona?**
Two years with the Registrar of Contractors, measured from close of escrow or occupancy on new construction, or from the date of the work on repairs and remodels. Your private contract rights can outlast that, but the state process does not.
