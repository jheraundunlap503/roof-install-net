# What Voids a Roof Warranty?

**You have two separate roof warranties, and almost everything written about voiding them blurs the difference. The manufacturer's product warranty covers material defects and generally cannot be cancelled by anything you do to your roof. The contractor's workmanship warranty covers installation and is the one that actually gets voided, usually by letting someone else work on the roof. In Arizona there is a third clock most homeowners never hear about: you have two years to file a complaint with the Registrar of Contractors, and after that the state cannot help you.**

Search this question and you get the same list on every site: satellite dishes, pressure washing, solar panels, skylights. Those lists are not exactly wrong, but they describe the wrong mechanism, and they leave out the deadline that matters most in Arizona.

## What are the two roof warranties you actually have?

Two documents, two different companies, two different failure modes.

[CertainTeed's own explanation](https://www.certainteed.com/inspiration/how-tos/my-roof-warranty-what-it-and-what-it-covers) puts it plainly: there is "the manufacturer's (product) warranty, which covers the product against defects in the materials," and "the roofing contractor's workmanship (installation) warranty, which covers workmanship errors by the installing roofing contractor."

Then comes the line that undercuts most of what you will read elsewhere. A product warranty, CertainTeed states, "is 'set in stone' by the manufacturer, and it cannot be altered by any actions of the homeowner or the contractor."

Read that again if you have been worried about your satellite dish.

## Can drilling into your roof really void the manufacturer warranty?

Not in the way the listicles suggest. Mounting a satellite dish does not cancel your material warranty. What it does is create damage that was never covered in the first place.

A product warranty covers the shingle or tile failing as a product: cracking, delamination, granule loss, premature deterioration. It does not cover a hole someone drilled. When a claim gets denied after a solar install, the denial is not "you voided your warranty," it is "this is not a material defect."

The practical difference matters. If your shingles start blistering across the whole south face, that claim survives regardless of what is bolted to your roof. [Owens Corning's warranty overview](https://www.owenscorning.com/en-us/roofing/warranty) sets out what its coverage tiers include, and the scope is about the product, not your behavior.

Where penetrations genuinely hurt you is the workmanship side, covered next. If you are planning panels, read our guide to [solar panels and roof replacement in Arizona](/blog/solar-panel-roof-replacement-arizona/) before anyone drills.

## What actually voids a workmanship warranty?

This is the warranty that really does evaporate, and the triggers are mundane:

- **Letting another contractor work on the roof.** The most common one by far. Your installer will not warrant work they did not control. A different crew mounting a dish, patching a leak, or installing a swamp cooler usually ends the workmanship coverage in that area or entirely.
- **Unpermitted structural changes.** New penetrations, added vents, or a rooftop unit installed outside the original scope.
- **Failure to maintain.** Most workmanship warranties require reasonable upkeep. Debris-clogged valleys and neglected flashing give an installer a defensible reason to decline.
- **Nonpayment or contract disputes.** A workmanship warranty is a contractual promise. If the contract is in dispute, so is the promise.

Because this warranty comes from a private company rather than a manufacturer, its terms vary wildly, from one year to lifetime. Get it in writing before work starts. Our guide to [reading a roofing warranty](/blog/how-to-read-roofing-warranty/) walks through the clauses that matter, and [roofing estimate versus contract](/blog/roofing-estimate-vs-contract/) covers what belongs in the paperwork.

## What does a manufacturer warranty exclude in Arizona?

Exclusions do more work than voiding ever does, and two of them are sharpened by Arizona's climate.

**Ventilation.** Nearly every manufacturer conditions coverage on adequate attic ventilation, because trapped heat cooks shingles from below. In a climate where the [NWS Phoenix office documents sustained extreme heat](https://www.weather.gov/psr/heat) every summer, this is not a technicality. Underventilated attics fail shingles early, and that failure gets attributed to the ventilation rather than the product. See [attic ventilation and your Arizona roof](/blog/attic-ventilation-roof-arizona/).

**Installation not per instructions.** Manufacturer coverage assumes the product went on the way the manufacturer specified. [Owens Corning's Duration installation instructions](https://www.owenscorning.com/en-us/roofing/install-instructions/duration-series) prescribe fastening patterns, joint gapping, and cold-weather hand-sealing. Deviations give a manufacturer grounds to route the claim back to your contractor, which is exactly why the workmanship warranty matters so much.

Weather damage is also excluded almost universally. Hail, wind above the rated threshold, and monsoon debris are insurance events, not warranty events. The [NRCA's technical resources](https://www.nrca.net/roofing-guidelines/resources) treat storm damage and product defect as separate categories, and so will your claims adjuster.

## Does registration or selling your home affect coverage?

Both, and this is where quiet coverage loss happens.

Enhanced or extended manufacturer warranties typically require registration within a set window after installation, often 60 to 90 days, and require installation by a certified contractor. Miss the registration and you silently fall back to the base limited warranty. Nobody sends a reminder.

Transfer rules bite at resale. Most warranties allow one transfer to a subsequent owner, sometimes only within a defined window and sometimes with a fee. A second transfer usually ends coverage. If you are buying a house with a recent roof, ask for the warranty documents and confirm the transfer was actually filed.

Standard limited warranties are also prorated. Full replacement value usually applies only during an initial period, after which the payout declines each year toward a small fraction of material cost, labor excluded.

## What recourse do you have in Arizona if a contractor will not honor workmanship?

A real one, and it expires.

The [Arizona Registrar of Contractors accepts formal complaints](https://roc.az.gov/file-complaint) against licensed contractors, and the deadline is strict. Per the ROC: for new construction, complaints "must be filed within two years from the close of escrow or actual occupancy, whichever occurred first." For remodel or repair work, the two years run from the date the work was performed.

That clock runs whether or not your contractor is still returning calls. A workmanship warranty advertised as ten years does not extend the state's two-year enforcement window; it only gives you a contract claim to pursue privately afterward.

Arizona also maintains a Residential Contractors' Recovery Fund with its own filing requirements, outlined in the [ROC's FAQ](https://roc.az.gov/faq). Both remedies require that your contractor was licensed at the time of the work, which is the strongest argument for verifying a license at [the ROC before you hire](https://roc.az.gov/before-hire) rather than after a dispute. Our guide to [Arizona roofing contractor licensing](/blog/arizona-roofing-contractor-license/) covers what to check.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does installing solar panels void my roof warranty?**
It does not cancel your manufacturer's product warranty, which covers material defects regardless. It very often ends your contractor's workmanship warranty for the affected area, because a different company penetrated the roof. Ask your roofer to do the mounting, or get written acknowledgment from both companies first.

**Does pressure washing void a roof warranty?**
Pressure washing can physically strip granules, and that damage is not a material defect, so a claim gets denied on scope rather than on voiding. Soft washing is the safer method. The outcome is the same either way: you pay.

**Can I put a second layer of shingles on and keep my warranty?**
Manufacturer coverage generally assumes installation over a proper deck, not over existing shingles, and many workmanship warranties exclude layovers outright. Beyond warranty terms, most Arizona jurisdictions limit total layers.

**Do I lose warranty coverage if I sell my house?**
Usually not immediately. Most warranties permit one transfer, often within a limited window and sometimes for a fee. The transfer has to actually be filed with the manufacturer, and that step is the one people skip.

**How long do I have to complain about bad roofing work in Arizona?**
Two years, per the Registrar of Contractors, measured from close of escrow or occupancy on new construction, or from the date of the work on repairs and remodels. Do not wait for a contractor to stop returning calls before filing.
