# How Much Does It Cost to Reroof a 2000 Sq Ft House?

**Reroofing a 2,000 square foot house in Arizona typically runs $8,000 to $18,000 for asphalt shingle and $12,000 to $25,000 for tile, with metal and premium materials higher. The single biggest variable most guides skip is whether your 2,000 square feet is one story or two, because that changes the actual roof size by half. This guide breaks down the real numbers, explains the roofing square, and shows what drives the price up or down.**

The "2,000 square foot house" is the most common way homeowners describe their home, but it is a measure of living space, not roof size. Those are not the same number, and confusing them is why online estimates vary so wildly. Before you can price a reroof, you have to translate living space into roof area.

Once you have the roof size, the material choice sets the range. In Arizona, that choice is shaped by heat, monsoon, and a UV index that runs 11 or higher for months, which is why tile and reflective materials are more common here than in most of the country.

## How much roof does a 2,000 sq ft house actually have?

It depends on the number of stories. A single-story 2,000 square foot home sits on roughly a 2,000 square foot footprint, so with pitch and overhangs the roof is about 2,200 to 2,600 square feet, or 22 to 26 roofing squares. A two-story 2,000 square foot home has half the footprint, so the roof is closer to 11 to 14 squares.

That difference can cut the cost of the same house nearly in half. A roofing square is 100 square feet of roof surface, the unit contractors use to order material and price labor. The [GAF explanation of what a roofing square is](https://www.gaf.com/en-us/blog/your-home/what-is-a-roofing-square-f955fe18-daf9-4b0c-b78e-28ca178caa35) is a useful primer. Roof area always exceeds floor area because the roof is sloped and extends past the walls, a point the [NRCA homeowner roofing resources](https://www.nrca.net/roofing-guidelines/resources) reinforce.

## What does it cost by material?

For a single-story 2,000 square foot Arizona home, expect roughly $8,000 to $18,000 for asphalt shingle, $12,000 to $25,000 for concrete or clay tile, $10,000 to $20,000 for metal, and $5,000 to $10,000 for a flat foam roof. A two-story home of the same living area lands lower because the roof is smaller.

Material is the largest cost lever. Manufacturer cost guidance from [GAF on what a new roof costs](https://www.gaf.com/en-us/plan-design/homeowner-education/roof-cost) and [Owens Corning on roof replacement cost](https://www.owenscorning.com/en-us/roofing/blog/roof-replacement-cost) reflects national ranges, and Arizona sits near the middle for shingle and above average for tile because tile is popular here. To compare the two most common Arizona choices, see [tile versus shingle in Arizona](/blog/tile-vs-shingle-roof-arizona/). You can also run your exact size and material through our [free roof cost estimator](/estimator/) for a local range.

## What drives the price up or down?

Beyond material and roof size, five factors move the number: labor, tear-off of the old roof, pitch and complexity, decking repairs, and permits. Each can shift the total by thousands, which is why two 2,000 square foot homes rarely get identical quotes.

Tear-off matters more than homeowners expect. Removing one existing layer adds cost, and a second layer adds more. Steep or cut-up roofs with valleys, dormers, and skylights raise both material waste and labor. If tear-off reveals rotted decking, that repair is extra and only shows up mid-job. The [GAF roof cost calculator](https://www.gaf.com/en-us/plan-design/homeowner-education/roof-cost/calculator) lets you test how these variables change the estimate. For a deeper breakdown, read [how roof cost scales by square footage in Arizona](/blog/roof-replacement-cost-by-square-footage-arizona/).

## Does an energy-efficient roof change the math?

A reflective cool roof costs a bit more up front but lowers cooling bills, which matters in Arizona's heat. The Department of Energy notes that cool roofs stay significantly cooler in the sun than standard roofs, cutting the heat that reaches your attic and air conditioner.

The savings are real but gradual. The [Department of Energy overview of cool roofs](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/articles/energy-101-video-cool-roofs-1) and its guidance on [energy-efficient cool roof products](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/purchasing-energy-efficient-cool-roof-products) explain the payback, and the [Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory cool roof fact sheet](https://heatisland.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2023-11/cool_roof_fact_sheet.pdf) quantifies how much surface temperature a reflective roof can shed. In a climate where roofs bake for months, the cooling savings can offset part of the higher material cost over the life of the roof.

## How to get an accurate reroof number

Get the roof measured, not estimated from your home's square footage, and collect at least three itemized quotes on the same scope. An accurate reroof price comes from actual roof area, your material, and a real look at the decking, not a rule of thumb.

Give every contractor the same specifications so you compare like for like: material, tear-off of all existing layers, underlayment, flashing, and warranty. Storm damage adds another wrinkle, and the [NRCA guidance on storm roof repairs](https://www.nrca.net/news-events/press-room/storm-roof-repairs) is worth reading before you file an insurance claim. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a 2,000 sq ft house the same as a 2,000 sq ft roof?**
No. That figure is living space. A single-story home has a roof of roughly 22 to 26 squares once pitch and overhangs are counted, while a two-story home of the same living area has about half that. Always price from roof area, not floor area.

**How many bundles of shingles for a 2,000 sq ft roof?**
Shingles come three bundles to a square. A single-story 2,000 square foot home at about 22 to 26 squares needs roughly 66 to 78 bundles plus waste, but the contractor should calculate this from a real measurement, not an online guess.

**Why is my quote higher than the online estimate?**
Online tools use floor area and average assumptions. A real quote reflects your actual roof size, pitch, number of existing layers to tear off, any decking repairs, and local labor. Tear-off and hidden decking damage are the two most common reasons a quote exceeds an online figure.

**Is tile worth the extra cost in Arizona?**
Tile costs more up front but lasts far longer than shingle in Arizona's UV load, often 30 to 50 years versus 15 to 20 for shingle. Whether the premium pays off depends on how long you plan to stay in the home.

**Does the price include removing the old roof?**
It should, but confirm it. Tear-off of the existing roof is standard on a reroof, yet some low quotes exclude it or assume a single layer. If your home has two layers, removal costs more, so ask each contractor to itemize it.

**How long does reroofing a 2,000 sq ft house take?**
Most single-family reroofs take one to three days depending on material, complexity, and weather. Tile and steep roofs take longer than a straightforward shingle job, and monsoon season can add delays.
