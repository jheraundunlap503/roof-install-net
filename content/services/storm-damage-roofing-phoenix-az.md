# Storm Damage Roofing in Phoenix, AZ

**Phoenix storm damage is overwhelmingly wind damage, not hail, and that changes how you document it. Wind is a covered peril on standard Arizona policies, but insurers dispute causation on older roofs, arguing deterioration rather than storm. The single strongest thing you can do is tie your damage to a specific dated National Weather Service event. The 2026 season gave the West Valley two, including an August 9 storm with a measured 84 mph gust and roof sections torn off.**

Most storm damage content is written for hail country, where damage is impact bruising you can photograph. Phoenix damage lifts, shifts, and peels, and frequently looks like nothing from the ground.

## What does storm damage look like on a Phoenix roof?

Five patterns, ordered by how often they get missed.

**Shifted tile without breakage.** Wind lifts and reseats tile without cracking it. The roof looks intact while underlayment sits exposed. This is the most commonly missed damage in the metro and it usually appears as a leak months later.

**Lifted or missing shingle tabs.** Broken adhesive seals leave shingles that look flat but are no longer bonded, so the next storm takes them.

**Flashing and edge damage.** Bent drip edge, separated wall flashing, displaced ridge caps.

**Debris impact.** Branches and airborne material puncturing tile or decking.

**Rooftop equipment displacement.** Evaporative coolers, satellite mounts, and solar attachments shift under wind loading and open a penetration.

## What did the 2026 season actually do?

Two documented events, both from the National Weather Service.

**August 9.** A [severe wind event](https://www.weather.gov/psr/August92026) tracked westward along Interstate 10 through Tolleson, Litchfield Park, Avondale, Goodyear, and Buckeye. Phoenix Terminal Doppler Radar sampled winds exceeding 70 to 80 mph, a weather station in Buckeye recorded an 84 mph peak gust, and Buckeye Municipal Airport recorded 73 mph. NWS documented "destroyed carports, blown-in garage doors, and roof parts blown off," with large trees uprooted, power poles snapped, and more than 50,000 SRP and APS customers losing power.

**August 12.** A separate system brought [damaging winds, a dust storm, and flash flooding](https://www.weather.gov/psr/August122026) to western Arizona, with radar-estimated rainfall of 2 to 4 inches around Quartzsite.

NWS maintains a running index at its [event summaries page](https://www.weather.gov/psr/eventsummaries). Check it against the date you believe your damage occurred, because an adjuster will.

## Why does causation decide your claim?

Because nobody disputes that damage exists. They dispute what caused it.

Wind is a covered peril. Wear is an exclusion. On a 20 year old Phoenix roof, an insurer can reasonably argue that tiles which shifted in August were already loose, or that underlayment failing now aged out rather than blew out. That argument is won or lost on documentation.

Three things make the case:

1. **Dated photographs**, taken as soon as possible after the event.
2. **The NWS event summary** for that date and your area.
3. **An independent inspection**, ideally before the adjuster's visit, from someone not bidding the repair.

## Should you file at all?

Not automatically, and this is where a contractor's advice and your interest diverge.

Run the deductible math first. Wind and hail deductibles are frequently a percentage of dwelling coverage rather than a flat amount, which on a typical Phoenix home can reach several thousand dollars. Damage costing $2,800 to repair is a repair, not a claim, and filing anyway puts a claim on your record with no payment against it.

File when damage is clearly storm-caused, clearly exceeds the deductible, and ties to a dated event. Pay out of pocket for a few tiles and a flashing detail.

## What should you do in the first week?

1. **Photograph everything** before any cleanup, with timestamps.
2. **Tarp active leaks.** Your policy requires mitigation, and reasonable mitigation costs are typically reimbursable. See [emergency roof repair in Phoenix](/services/emergency-roof-repair-phoenix-az/).
3. **Note the NWS event date** for your area.
4. **Get an independent inspection**, not just a contractor quote.
5. **Open the claim** if the damage warrants it.

Do not walk the roof yourself. Wet or heat-softened tile breaks underfoot, and damage you cause is not storm damage.

## How do you avoid storm chasers in Phoenix?

They arrive within days of exactly the kind of event the West Valley saw in August, and Phoenix is the largest market they target.

Verify licensing at the [Arizona Registrar of Contractors](https://roc.az.gov/before-hire) and confirm the [classification covers roofing](https://roc.az.gov/license-classifications). Refuse to sign an assignment of insurance benefits at the door. Treat any offer to waive or absorb your deductible as what it is, a request to participate in insurance fraud, and a reliable signal about the rest of the operation.

Legitimate Phoenix roofers are booked solid after a major storm. Immediate availability is information. See [storm chaser roofers in Arizona](/blog/storm-chaser-roofer-arizona/) and [Arizona roofing scams](/blog/roofing-scams-arizona/). If work goes wrong, the [ROC complaint process](https://roc.az.gov/file-complaint) requires the contractor to have been licensed at the time and imposes a two year filing deadline.

## Does repair or replacement follow storm damage?

It depends on what the storm exposed, and the answer is usually about underlayment.

Localized damage on a roof with sound underlayment is a repair. Storm damage on a roof already near end of life often accelerates a replacement that was coming anyway, and insurers will pay for the storm-caused portion rather than the whole roof. The [NRCA's technical resources](https://www.nrca.net/roofing-guidelines/resources) treat underlayment as the actual water barrier, which is why its condition governs.

Phoenix exempts like-for-like re-roofing from permitting per the [city's published exemption list](https://www.phoenix.gov/pddsite/Documents/TRT/dsd_trt_pdf_00823.pdf), so no municipal inspection catches decking damage during a routine re-roof. Outside city limits, [unincorporated Maricopa County](https://www.maricopa.gov/1674/Residential-Construction) rules apply. See [roof repair versus replacement](/blog/roof-repair-vs-replacement/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does Phoenix get hail damage?**
Occasionally, but wind is the dominant storm damage mechanism in the low desert. Hail bruises and fractures; monsoon wind lifts, shifts, and peels. They are inspected and documented differently. See [hail damage on Arizona roofs](/blog/hail-damage-roof-arizona/).

**How do I prove a storm caused my roof damage?**
Dated photographs plus the NWS event summary for that date and location, ideally supported by an independent inspection. A documented 84 mph gust near your address on the date of your photos is far stronger than a general account of a storm.

**How long do I have to file a storm damage claim in Arizona?**
Policies set their own notice requirements, often measured in days, and delay is a common denial reason. Report promptly even if undecided about repairs. Reporting is not committing.

**Will insurance replace my whole roof after storm damage?**
Sometimes, when damage is widespread or matching materials is impossible. Frequently they pay for the damaged portion instead. Age and pre-existing condition drive that determination.

**Should I use the contractor my insurer recommends?**
You are not required to. Preferred vendors are often fine but maintain an ongoing relationship with the insurer rather than with you. Verify them through the ROC like anyone else.
