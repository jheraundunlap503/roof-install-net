# Roof Inspection in Phoenix, AZ

**Most Phoenix roof inspections are free, and that is the problem: a free inspection is a sales visit that ends in a quote. An independent paid inspection generally runs in the low hundreds and produces a report you can use against an insurer or a seller. Either way, the finding that matters on a Phoenix tile roof is not the tile. It is the age and condition of the underlayment beneath it, and any inspection that does not lift tile to look has not answered the question you asked.**

Phoenix roofs fail from underneath. Tile and shingle take the visible wear, while heat degrades the waterproof layer nobody sees until it leaks.

## What does a roof inspection cost in Phoenix?

Two different products with the same name.

**Free contractor inspection.** Offered by most roofing companies. Genuinely useful for a second opinion or a repair quote, and legitimate companies do real work on them. The inspector is also the person who profits from the recommendation, which is worth holding in mind when the recommendation is a full replacement.

**Independent paid inspection.** Generally low hundreds of dollars. The inspector is not bidding the work, which is precisely why the report carries more weight in an insurance dispute or a real estate negotiation.

**Insurance or real estate roof certification.** Priced higher and scoped to a specific form or lender requirement.

Pay for the independent one when money turns on the answer. Take the free one when you want a repair quote.

## What should a Phoenix inspection actually cover?

Seven areas. A report missing the third item is not an inspection of a Phoenix roof.

1. Tile or shingle condition, including shifted-but-unbroken tile.
2. Flashing at walls, valleys, chimneys, and penetrations.
3. **Underlayment condition and estimated remaining life**, assessed by lifting tile, not inferred from the surface.
4. Rooftop equipment, especially evaporative cooler curbs, satellite mounts, and solar attachments.
5. Drainage, scuppers, and any ponding on low-slope sections.
6. Attic: daylight through the deck, staining on rafters, damp insulation, blocked soffit vents.
7. Photographs of every finding, taken on the roof.

## Why is underlayment the only finding that really matters?

Because on a Phoenix tile roof it is the actual water barrier, and it fails decades before the tile does.

Tile lasts 30 to 50 years. Underlayment typically fails at 15 to 25 here. The [NWS Phoenix office documents the sustained extreme heat](https://www.weather.gov/psr/heat) that drives it, and attic and deck temperatures run well above ambient all summer. The [NRCA's technical resources](https://www.nrca.net/roofing-guidelines/resources) treat underlayment as a defined service-life component rather than a permanent one.

The practical consequence: a Phoenix home can have a visually perfect tile roof and still need a full re-roof. An inspector who reports "tile is in good condition" and stops there has told you nothing about whether your roof is watertight. See [why underlayment matters](/blog/roofing-underlayment-importance/).

## When should you get an inspection?

Five triggers, and two of them are seasonal.

- **Before monsoon season**, ideally in May. See our [pre-monsoon inspection guide](/blog/pre-monsoon-roof-inspection-arizona/).
- **After monsoon season**, in October, or after any significant storm. The [season runs June 15 through September 30](https://www.weather.gov/fgz/monsooninfo).
- **Before buying or listing a home.**
- **Before a solar installation**, because mounting panels on a roof near end of life is an expensive sequencing error.
- **At 15 years**, then annually, regardless of appearance.

After a documented storm, timing matters for a different reason. An inspection dated close to an [NWS-documented event](https://www.weather.gov/psr/August92026) is far stronger evidence of causation than a description offered weeks later.

## Does an inspection help with an insurance claim?

Substantially, and mostly on the question insurers actually fight about.

Wind is a covered peril. Wear is not. On an older Phoenix roof the dispute is almost never whether damage exists, it is whether a storm caused it or the roof simply aged out. An independent inspection establishes condition and timing before an adjuster forms a view, and it comes from someone with no financial interest in the repair.

## What should the written report contain?

Six things, and you should ask for them before booking:

1. Photographs of every issue, taken on the roof rather than from the ground.
2. Underlayment condition with estimated remaining life.
3. Ventilation assessment, since it is a warranty condition on most manufacturer coverage.
4. Flashing and penetration condition itemized by location.
5. A repair-versus-replace recommendation with stated reasoning.
6. Inspection date and the inspector's license number.

A one-page "roof needs replacement" with no photographs and no underlayment assessment is a quote wearing a report's clothing.

## How do you vet a Phoenix roof inspector?

Verify the license at the [Arizona Registrar of Contractors](https://roc.az.gov/before-hire) and confirm the [classification covers roofing](https://roc.az.gov/license-classifications), since a general residential license does not.

Ask two questions that separate real inspections from sales calls. Did you lift tile to look at the underlayment, and what did you see? Will I get photographs whether or not I hire you? An inspector who answers both well is doing the job.

Note that Phoenix exempts like-for-like re-roofing from permitting per the [city's published exemption list](https://www.phoenix.gov/pddsite/Documents/TRT/dsd_trt_pdf_00823.pdf), which means no municipal inspection of your decking happens during a routine re-roof. Outside city limits, [unincorporated Maricopa County](https://www.maricopa.gov/1674/Residential-Construction) rules apply. That absence is an argument for paying for your own inspection rather than relying on one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does a roof inspection cost in Phoenix, AZ?**
Contractor inspections are usually free and end in a quote. Independent paid inspections generally run in the low hundreds and produce a report from someone not bidding the work.

**Is a free roof inspection worth getting?**
Yes for a repair quote or a second opinion. Be cautious when a free inspection concludes with an urgent full-replacement recommendation and a discount that expires.

**How often should a Phoenix roof be inspected?**
Annually after 15 years, plus before and after monsoon season, and after any significant storm.

**Will an inspector walk on my tile roof?**
A competent one will, carefully, and will lift tile at selected points to assess underlayment. Someone who inspects a tile roof only from the ground or a ladder cannot tell you what you need to know.

**Do I need an inspection before installing solar in Phoenix?**
Yes. Panels have a service life far longer than aging underlayment, and removing and reinstalling an array to re-roof underneath is a significant avoidable cost.
